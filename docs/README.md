# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a customer-first, iterative project management approach with clear ownership, measurable outcomes, and continuous improvement. Its lifecycle moves from initiation and stakeholder alignment through planning, execution, release, and retrospective improvement. Project teams maintain shared artifacts—including the project charter or one-pager, roadmap, backlog, acceptance criteria, Definition of Done, risk register, release notes, and retrospective actions—to keep decisions and progress visible.

Projects begin by validating the business need, defining a SMART objective and success metrics, identifying stakeholders, and confirming the decision to proceed. During planning, the team turns the approved initiative into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, owners, dependencies, milestones, a release plan, and an agreed Definition of Done. During execution, work is tracked on a project board, delivery is organized into small increments, and progress is reviewed through regular standups, weekly delivery or PM/PdM syncs, and sprint or milestone demos.

OctoAcme assigns clear responsibilities across project and product managers, developers, QA/testing, and stakeholders. Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, dependencies, communications, and documentation; Product Managers define outcomes, prioritize the backlog, and measure customer and business value; developers implement and test maintainable solutions; QA validates quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Risks are recorded, assessed, mitigated, and reviewed regularly, with escalation from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor when business impact requires it. Status updates use a shared source of truth and communicate progress, next steps, blockers, risks, and decisions needed.

Quality assurance is continuous from planning through deployment. New logic requires unit tests, with integration tests where appropriate, end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows, CI checks for tests and linting, security scanning, and manual QA when needed. Releases require completed acceptance criteria, passing CI and security checks, release notes, a rollback or mitigation plan, staging smoke tests, production verification, and stakeholder communication. After each sprint, release, milestone, or incident, retrospectives capture what went well, what could improve, and a small number of owned, time-bound action items that are tracked through the backlog or issues.
